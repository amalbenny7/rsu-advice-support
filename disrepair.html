Disrepair

This information relates to privately rented accommodation and sets out the responsibilities of landlords and tenants in relation to disrepair.

What is your landlord responsible for?

What is disrepair?

Disrepair is when your home or something in your home is in need of repair. To be defined as disrepair there has to have been some deterioration from a former state.

The Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 states that the landlord is responsible for ensuring that the following areas are kept in a good state of repair:

  • The structure and exterior of the property
  • Bath, sinks, basins and other sanitary fittings including pipes and drains
  • Heating and hot water installations
  • Gas appliances and electricity supply and meters
  • Pipes, flues and ventilations
  • Common areas
  • Any damage they cause through attempting repairs
Common areas

Common areas relate to areas or structures that are used or accessed by the tenant but are not within the room or flat rented by the tenant. These could include stairs and hallways.

Houses in Multiple Occupation (HMO) properties

The Management of Houses in Multiple Occupation (England) Regulations 2006 state that landlords of Houses in Multiple Occupation (HMO) have additional responsibilities in relation to repairs and health and safety.

It is local authorities that issue HMO licences. More information on Licences, can be found here: Houses in multiple occupation (Reading Borough Council)

Furniture and appliances

There is an implied term in tenancy agreements that furnished premises will be fit for habitation from the start of the letting. The Consumer Rights Act 2015 states that goods that are supplied under the contract must be fit for purpose, safe and durable from the start of the tenancy. Furniture and furnishings supplied by the landlord must meet The Furniture and Furnishings (Fire Safety) Regulations. If there is an inventory of items. It is the landlord’s responsibility to fix broken furniture supplied in the tenancy agreement. Repair built-in or provided appliances like fridges and washing machines – And maintain the roof, pipes, heating, and electric wiring. Landlords should handle repairs quickly after you report the issue.

Landlords would not be responsible for general wear and tear or damages caused by a tenant or guests of the tenant.

Damp and mould

Damp is moisture which affects your house. It can damage the property and can have an impact on your health. How do I know if my house has damp?

Signs of damp include:

  • Mould growth
  • Patches of damp which get darker when it rains
  • Running water on windows and walls
  • Crumbling plaster
  • Peeling paint and wallpaper
  • Decaying skirting boards and floors

Damp is caused by a variety of reasons which include a lack of ventilation and humid living conditions. This may be a result of lifestyle choices, like drying clothes indoors, or structural issues, like poor quality repairs.

What are you responsible for?

It is a tenant’s responsibility to take care of the property and use it in a responsible way. It would be reasonable to expect a tenant to change light bulbs when they fail and unblock sinks that have been blocked by food waste etc. A tenant would be responsible for repair if the damage resulted from their actions or the actions of their guests in the property. Tenants should take care to prevent mould and damp; ensuring proper ventilation of the property.

Disrepair and your tenancy agreement

Your tenancy agreement may set out the responsibilities of the landlord and tenant in relation to repairs: These are known as express terms. A landlord cannot include any terms that reduce the tenant’s legal rights in relation to repairs. If there are no written terms in relation to repairs within the tenancy agreement, then section 11 of the Landlord and Tenancy Act 1985 should be used. This section requires landlords to be responsible for the repair of the areas and structures mentioned above. This may be referred to as implied terms of a tenancy agreement.

Reporting a problem

It is advised that you report any problems to your landlord as soon as you are aware of them. If possible, this should be in writing so you have proof that you have informed your landlord of the issue. If it is urgent and you need to phone your landlord, it is advised that you follow this up in writing. A landlord is not responsible for repairs until they have been notified of the problem. Most landlords will behave reasonably and resolve any repair issues highlighted by a tenant.

More information can be found here: How to report repairs to a private landlord (Shelter)

Do not withhold your rent

You should not withhold your rent due to disrepair or landlord failure to resolve repairs. This would weaken any claim you may have in relation to the disrepair and would also make it easier for your landlord to evict you.

If the repairs are not done

If your landlord does not carry out necessary repairs, work through the steps below. If you would like further advice on what your options are, please contact the Student Union Advice Service for support.

  1. Report it in writing

    Inform the landlord in writing of the repair, i.e., email, WhatsApp or text. Allow the landlord a reasonable period to respond and resolve (or begin to resolve) the issue. Some further information and templates can be found here: How to report repairs to a private landlord (Shelter)

  2. Make a formal complaint

    If your landlord doesn’t complete the repairs required, make a formal complaint to your landlord in writing marking this document ‘Formal Compliant’ so there can be no dispute over what the document is. The document should include:

    • The issue(s) you are making a complaint about
    • dates and times the problem took place
    • details of any conversations with your landlord and what they agreed to do
    • copies of any letters or emails between you and your landlord
    • photographs - for example showing damage or disrepair, particularly if the problem gets worse over time
    • receipts for things you've had to pay for because of the problem - for example, laundry bills if your washing machine broke down
    • a note from your doctor (if applicable) - if the problem has affected your health or safety
    • what you want them to do about the problem to resolve the matter

    Allow the landlord a reasonable time to respond, 14 days is acceptable.

  3. Contact Reading Borough Council Housing Enforcement

    If the issue is not resolved or you are ignored, the next step is to contact Reading Borough Council Housing Enforcement, who work in partnership with the Reading Students’ Union Advice Service. The Housing Enforcement Team believe that all tenants deserve ‘quiet enjoyment of their home’ and is looking to help students who may need professional help to enforce against poor conditions in their properties and rogue landlords.

    RBC Housing Enforcement deals with a variety of issues. Officers can support discussions with your landlord and negotiate solutions, and they also have enforcement powers. Issues such as

    • Disrepair and hazards in privately rented properties e.g. damp and mould, electrical issues, no hot water, fire alarms in disrepair where the landlord has not taken action.
    • Issues regarding pest activity e.g. rats, cockroaches.
    • Unfair Terms in Contracts
    • Harassment and aggressive practices

    If you wish to find out more information, visit their webpage at the Reading Borough Council website here: Private renting (Reading Borough Council)

    Contact information

    Telephone: 0118 937 3787
    Email: hmo@reading.gov.uk

  4. Ask about a redress scheme

    If you remain unsatisfied, you can ask your landlord/agent if they belong to a housing scheme such as The Housing Ombudsman or the Property Redress Scheme.

    More information on schemes can be found here: How to complain about your property agent (Which?)

Talk to an adviser

If you would like further advice or to speak with somebody in person then a Students’ Union Adviser may be able to help. The specialist advisers offer a confidential service, independent from the University and can discuss your case with you in confidence.

Leaving your tenancy

This information is for students living in Private Rented Accommodation, as halls accommodation is not subject to the Renters Rights Act.

Giving notice

Most students living in privately rented accommodation will have signed an Assured Periodic Tenancy (APT) Agreement – Post May 2026. This means that the agreement is rolling from month to month with no fixed end date. The agreement is between the landlord and the sole/joint tenants.

To leave the tenancy, you must give 2 months’ notice at the appropriate time. If you have a joint tenancy. It only requires one tenant to submit a Notice to Quit (NTQ), and it will be accepted. However, by submitting the NTQ, this will be accepted on behalf of all the tenants and the tenancy will finish for all tenants who are named on the contract.

If you want to leave a contract mid-way through the academic year, we advise you to speak to your landlord and fellow housemates to see if a resolution can be found.

Ending the tenancy before you have moved into the property

Even though you have not moved into the property, it is likely that you will still have signed a contractual agreement which means that you have agreed to rent the property.

You would be liable for the rent until such a time when you can submit a Notice to Quit (NTQ) offering the landlord two months’ notice – those months and associated housing costs you are liable to pay.

If you cannot move into the property, talk to your landlord or letting agent. You, your housemates, your landlord or agent may know someone who wants to move in, and take your place on the contract.

Once you have moved into the property

After you have moved into the property, there are several things which you need to take into consideration if you decide to move out:

What does your tenancy agreement say?

What does your tenancy agreement say that you can and cannot do? As you will have signed a tenancy agreement at the start of your tenancy, this is likely to set out the circumstances under which you can leave the property.

Who wants to move out?

The situation may be different if all joint tenants decide that they want to leave a property, or if just one person wants to leave. If only one person wants to leave the tenancy - In most cases, the best solution when a joint tenancy is to discuss this with your housemates and landlord. The best outcome is if everyone agrees, and a replacement can be found. If there is an option of assignment if a suitable alternative tenant has been found, as one tenant leaves and is replaced by another. The remaining tenants, together with the outgoing tenant, assign the tenancy to the remaining tenants and any new incoming occupant.

Finding an alternative tenant

It is the responsibility of the tenants, not the landlord, to find someone to take over the tenancy. Landlords and agents often charge a fee to accept a surrender of tenancy and accept an alternative tenant. Advice Service does not keep a list of students looking for accommodation, but you could consider advertising spare rooms in houses or enquiring about the availability of rooms using:

  • “Find a Housemate – Reading Students’ Union” Facebook Group
  • “Find a Housemate” Noticeboard (located in the Students’ Union building on Whiteknights campus)
Issuing a Notice to Quit on a joint contract

Under the Renters Rights Act, tenants in a joint contract can issue the landlords with a Notice to Quit (NTQ) for their tenancy in line with regulations. However, one person submitting a Notice to Quit (NQT) doesn’t mean only one person leaves the tenancy – it cancels the tenancy for all joint tenants. Please think carefully before handing in notice and speak with your housemates and landlord.

If all joint tenants want to leave a property, then, under an assured periodic tenancy, you can issue your landlord a Notice to Quit (NTQ) – giving 2 months’ notice.

Other ways of ‘escaping’ a tenancy

There is different legislation that supports housing and contract law. However, these can rely on complex housing law. These are:

  • Fundamental Breach of Contract;
  • The Contractual Remedies of Misrepresentation;
  • Consumer Protection Legislation concerning the “right to unwind”.

Moving out

Under the Renters Rights Act, tenants must give a minimum of 2 months’ notice to leave a tenancy.

Planning your move will help make the process easier and less stressful. It will also help increase your chances of getting your full deposit back at the end of your tenancy. Your landlord can make deductions from your deposit for damage or extra cleaning costs, so do not leave the cleaning until the last minute.

Make every effort to leave the inside and outside of the premises in a clean and tidy state. You should leave the property and its contents in the same condition as it was let to you, allowing for reasonable wear and tear through normal use.

Once you have cleaned up try to arrange with your landlord or agent to meet you to look over the property before you move out. This may give you the chance to re-clean areas the landlord is not happy with. Just before you move out, you should then arrange for a final inventory when the house is empty and all cleaning has been done.

When you leave your property your landlord or agent may try to implement fees, charges or services from your deposit. Under the Tenants Fee Act 2019 there are only certain things that a landlord/agent can lawfully charge you for.

Some helpful information can be found here: Tenant fees and other costs (Shelter)

Student tenancies in an HMO: Ground 4A

If your property is a House of Multiple Occupancy, your landlord can ask for it back over the summer using a Ground 4A notice, even though your contract is rolling. See Ground 4A on our Renters’ Rights Act page.

Talk to an adviser

If you would like further advice or to speak with somebody in person then a Students’ Union Adviser may be able to help. The specialist advisers offer a confidential service, independent from the University and can discuss your case with you in confidence.

Private rental

A guide to renting privately: what to work out before you start looking, what happens at each stage once you have found a property, and what you will be asked to pay and sign along the way.

Before you start looking

Budget

Decide how much you can afford to spend on housing and utilities - as these are often paid separately to rent - before you start your search. Don’t forget to budget for food, toiletries, cleaning materials, travel, books, clothing… if you need some help with budgeting, you can find lots of on-line tools to help you.

Where to look for a property

There are different place to find properties:

  • Use the internet to find local agents in the area.
  • The ‘Find a Housemate’ and ‘Find a Landlord’ notice boards, located in the Reading Students’ Union building on Whiteknights Campus;
  • Asking fellow students, if they are already local and/or on campus;
  • If looking for sole occupancy you may wish to join the Facebook group ‘Find a Housemate – Reading Students’ Union’. This is where students advertise any room(s) available in their current properties, so you would be joining a ‘ready formed’ house;
  • Internet sites such as spareroom.com or local free add internet directories (but - be aware of scams on these sites – check with the Advice Service is you are worried about scams).

What happens once I have found a property that I like and can afford?

  1. View the property

    You don’t have to take the first one you see; you can look at more than one. If you are overseas when you start your search, you might want to ask the landlord or agent for a virtual tour of the property.

  2. Nominate a lead tenant and pay the holding deposit

    If you wish to take the property (as part of a group) you will be asked to nominate a lead tenant. This doesn’t mean the lead tenant takes responsibility for all rental payments; as a joint tenancy, you are all equally liable. The lead tenant is the point of contact for the agent or landlord. If you are occupying on your own, you will be the lead (and only) tenant. You (or the group) will be asked for a Holding Deposit, equivalent to 1 week’s rental cost. This holding deposit will become part of the deposit for the property. This is often non-refundable, so be sure you want to take the property before you pay it, because it is unlikely it will be repaid if you change your mind about the property. The agent will ask the lead or sole tenant to sign a reservation form to confirm that the group has accepted the property and has paid the Holding Deposit.

    More information on holding deposits can be found here: Holding deposits (Shelter)

  3. Complete the paperwork

    You will be asked to complete the following paperwork:

    • An application form
    • Provide a copy of your student ID (letter of acceptance)
    • Passport
    • If you are an international student, in addition you will also be asked for:
    • A copy of your visa

    The landlord and/or agent is bound by law to carry out a ‘Right to Rent’ check on all tenants, and some of this information is required for this check. More information about the Right to Rent checks can be found here: Right to Rent immigration checks (Shelter)

  4. Provide a guarantor

    As a student you will be asked to provide a UK guarantor. This means finding a UK person whom is employed, earning a salary and is a home owner who will sign a legal document to say that they will underwrite the costs of the rental payments or any damage caused to the property should you as the tenant do not – or refuse to - pay.

    If you do not have any persons in the UK willing to be a guarantor for you or you are unable to get a UK based guarantor, then your options would depend on the landlord. The most likely alternative to a UK based personal guarantor is to find a UK based guarantor company – see Guarantors below.

  5. Sign the tenancy agreement

    Steps 5, 6 and 7 are often completed on the same day.

    Once you have returned all the paperwork in a timely manner you will be asked to sign the Tenancy Agreement. You must always read this agreement in full: it is a legal document and by signing you are agreeing to the terms and conditions stated within it.

  6. Pay the deposit

    You will be asked to pay the remaining amount of the (damage/security) deposit for the property (minus your holding deposit - this will be deducted from the deposit as already paid). The maximum charge is 5 weeks’ rental cost.

    The deposit must be put into one of three UK government-backed deposit schemes within 30 days of you paying the money, and ‘prescribed information’ must be given to the tenant. More information about the ‘prescribed information’ can be found here: Information landlords must give tenants (GOV.UK)

    You will be notified by email of your deposit certificate. It is important to keep this safe, as you may need it if you want to challenge any deductions to the deposit at the end of the tenancy. If you do not receive a deposit certificate or the ‘prescribed information’ within 30 days of paying your deposit, then please get in contact with your landlord/agent or the Student Union Advice Service.

    Most agents/landlords will provide the tenants with a welcome pack with all relevant paperwork. You must keep this information safe. More information about your deposit can be found here: Check your tenancy deposit is protected (Shelter)

  7. Check the prescribed information

    There is some information which the landlord or agent must provide you with, called prescribed information, which includes:

    • Written Statement / Tenancy Terms
    • A copy of the Energy Performance Certificate
    • A copy of the gas safety certificate
    • A copy of the electrical safety certificate

  8. Pay the first month’s rent

    You will be asked to pay the first month’s rent before you move into your new property. Rent is paid in advance: if you pay your rent on 1st of every month, you have paid to live in your property for that month. You would not live in the property and then pay at the end of the month for living in the property.

  9. Check the inventory

    When moving in you should receive an inventory. An inventory is a document made up of pictures and words describing the condition of the property and the items in the property. It is really important that you check this document very carefully to ensure that the descriptions are accurate. You have 7 days from the start of the tenancy to make any amendments and return to your landlord and/or agent.

    The inventory taken when you become responsible for the property helps keep your deposit safe and secure.

    When you leave the property, another inventory will be taken. The moving out inventory can be compared with the moving in inventory, and used to highlight any damage caused by the tenants to the property, and the landlord can seek to remove funds from your deposit for the damage caused by the tenant.

  10. Sort out the bills

    If Bills are not included, you will need to sort out any bills that you will need to pay whilst living in the property. This includes the utilities such as gas, electric and water, and also any internet you wish to have, and the TV Licence.

    If your utilities are not included in your monthly rental income, you will be responsible for paying for these services for the length of the tenancy. Many agents or landlords would notify the current providers of the new tenants and the date they move in. However, you can change providers in most cases. Should your landlord not allow you too, please speak with the Advice Service.

    Utilities can be a large expense so it can be good to shop around for the best deals available. Some useful website can be found here:

    • Citizens advice – compare domestic energy suppliers
    • Uswitch – www.uswitch.com
    • Money saving expert – www.moneysavingexpert.com

Deposits, guarantors and Council Tax

Deposits

Once you decide that you want to take a property, and pay the ‘Holding Deposit’ payment, you will need to pay a deposit towards the property. This is held in one of 3 government approved scheme. The amount of the deposit can be up to 5 weeks’ rental value. More information on the schemes can be found here: Deposit protection schemes and landlords (GOV.UK)

Once you pay this to the agent/landlord. They have 30 days to deposit the money into the scheme, and likely that the ‘Lead Tenant’ will receive a certificate via email. Stating where the deposit is being held. This document is important, at the end of your tenancy.

A holding deposit is money you pay to a landlord or letting agent to reserve a property before you sign a tenancy agreement. Landlords and agents cannot take more than the cost of 1 week’s rent for the property as a holding deposit.

Guarantors

Students will generally be asked to provide a guarantor. A guarantor will underwrite the costs of the rental payments or any damage caused to the property should you as the tenant, not pay your rent or refuse to pay for damages caused.

Guarantors generally need to be a UK citizen who is employed, earning a salary and/or is a home owner who will sign a legal document to say to say they will accept liability for your rental fees.

Under new Renters Rights Legislation, Landlords and Agents are limited to accepting just one month's rent in advance, along with a deposit. You can pay more rent in advance, if you want to, but the landlord can no longer ask for a tenant to pay more than a month’s rent in advance.

If you do not have any persons in the UK willing to be a guarantor for you, or you are unable to get a UK based guarantor, then your options would depend on the landlord. The most likely alternative to a UK based guarantor is a guarantor company.

There are student guarantor companies, who, for a fee, will underwrite the costs. It is best to speak with your landlord or agent if you are struggling to pass the required checks they are asking for.

Reading Student Union is in partnership with Housing Hand, a 3rd party guarantor company. Housing Hand who can provide students with a company guarantor. To take advantage of the special rates, offered to University of Reading Student, please use the link below: Housing Hand – Reading Students’ Union rates

Council Tax

Council Tax is a local tax paid by residents to the local council. It contributes towards things like refuge collection, policing, fire services and the maintenance of outdoor areas such as public garden and parks in the local area.

As a full-time student you will be exempt from Council Tax, and if everyone in your house hold is a student then no one in the property should pay Council Tax. However, there may also be periods, when - even as a student - you may be liable for Council Tax payments, such as any period (which could be days, weeks or months) outside your registered course dates.

As an example if you arrive in the UK and rent a property from 1st of August and your course doesn’t start official until 20th of September the period between 1st of August and 19th of September, you are not a student – so will be liable for Council Tax. The same applies to the period when your course ends but you are still in a tenancy agreement for a private rented property.

For exemptions, local authority contact details and what to do if a bill arrives, see Council Tax in our Money advice.

Private halls

Both university and private halls have different priced rooms, based on the size and amities that they have in them. Contract lengths will also vary – normally between 40 & 50 weeks.

You may require a guarantor, and international guarantors may be accepted. You will pay a deposit. This will be returned providing that you leave the room in a good condition.

Bills are generally included within the price and generally are located either walking distance from universities or on public transport routes to universities.

There may be catered for options offered with halls accommodation, shared kitchens, and you purchase your own food, or a private kitchen within your room/apartment – pending on room type.

Halls are not covered by the Renters’ Rights Act

Student halls providers are exempt from the Renters Rights Act, meaning that once you sign a contract with a provider, you are liable for the contract for its entire length, unless your provider allows you to find a replacement student tenant.

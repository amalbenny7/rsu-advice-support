Searching for accommodation

There are five kinds of student accommodation in Reading. This page explains how each one works, what to weigh up before you commit, and who the providers are – so you can compare your options before you sign anything.

Get advice →

Information, not a recommendation

The Advice Service cannot comment on the suitability or quality of any accommodation or accommodation provider listed on this page. Inclusion here should not be taken as a recommendation by Reading Students’ Union or the Advice Service.

Please consider your options carefully and only enter into an agreement or sign a contract when you are happy to do so. Contracts are legally binding agreements.

Your options

Start with the option that fits how long you need somewhere to live and how much you want settled before you arrive. You can jump straight to any of them.

  • University accommodation → Halls of Residence on campus, run by the University. Agreements usually run for an academic year and are paid in termly instalments.
  • Private halls of residence → Halls around Reading operated by private companies rather than the University. Usually let on a tenancy agreement, with a deposit paid when you book.
  • Private rented accommodation → A property rented on your own or with others, direct from a landlord or through a letting agent. Fifteen local agents are listed.
  • Temporary accommodation → Somewhere to stay short term – a hotel, guest house or bed and breakfast – while you look for something permanent.
  • Homestay → Staying with a local host family, arranged through an external supplier. Your rights are different to other kinds of renting.

University accommodation

The University has Halls of Residence which are on campus. Information on the Halls of Residence can be found at Accommodation (reading.ac.uk). There are separate sections for prospective and returning students, as well as for staff and visitors.

Some things to consider

  • Halls of Residence agreements usually last for an academic year: this means that you will sign an agreement to say that you will remain in the accommodation for a set period of time. It is unlikely that you will be able to leave within this period; if you do, you may still be liable to pay rent.
  • Halls of Residence are paid for in termly instalments.
  • Some Halls of Residence packages are catered. With a catered package, you are provided with food in addition to accommodation. The term of this agreement is usually 31 weeks, rather than for the whole period (either 40 or 51 weeks).
  • There is some accommodation which can accommodate families, however the amount available is limited and can only accommodate a maximum of two children under the age of twelve. Further information can be found on the University website: Room types (reading.ac.uk).
  • There is less availability for returning students. This means that if you are progressing onto the second, third or subsequent year of your course, you may not be allocated a place in Halls.
  • If you would like to stay in halls after the end of your 40-week contract, Stay@Reading offers halls accommodation during the summer. See Stay@Reading (reading.ac.uk) for more information.

Private halls of residence

There are a number of Halls of Residence within Reading which are operated by private companies rather than by the University.

Some things to consider

  • Private halls of residence tenants should be given a tenancy agreement which would usually – though not always – be an Assured Shorthold Tenancy agreement. This means you are unlikely to be able to leave the accommodation early without having to pay the rent for the duration of your agreement.
  • You may not be able to choose the people you will be living and sharing facilities with.
  • Extra costs: factor in any up-front or additional costs. For example, you will pay a deposit when you book your room, rent is usually paid in instalments, and you may need a TV Licence if you watch TV in your room.
  • As private halls are located in various places around Reading, you may need to consider travel to and from the campus.

Private rented accommodation

Privately rented accommodation is available throughout Reading. This involves renting a property, either on your own or with other students or individuals, directly from a private landlord or through a letting agency.

Before you pay anything

Be aware of scams, particularly on free advertising sites and social media. If you are worried that a property or a landlord might not be genuine, check with the Advice Service before you hand over any money.

Where else to look

  • Accommodation search websites – for example Rightmove or Zoopla – can help with searches across letting agencies.
  • The Reading Chronicle is a local newspaper which advertises properties for rent.
  • Students often advertise spare rooms in privately rented houses on the Facebook page ‘Reading University Student Union Find a Housemate’. There is also a noticeboard in the Reading Students’ Union building on Whiteknights Campus where students can advertise for housemates.
  • The ‘Find a Landlord’ noticeboard in the Reading Students’ Union building on Whiteknights Campus, where landlords advertise directly to students.

Checking an agent

By law all letting agents are required to belong to one of the following redress schemes:

  • The Property Ombudsman
  • Ombudsman Services Property
  • The Property Redress Scheme

Letting agents can also belong voluntarily to accreditation schemes:

  • Association of Residential Letting Agents (ARLA) Propertymark
  • National Association of Estate Agents (NAEA) Propertymark
  • National Approved Lettings Scheme (NALS)

Fees

  • By law letting agents should clearly advertise compulsory fees from the outset, and are prevented from charging fees until a property has been identified and accepted by the tenant. It is also illegal for fees to be charged for supplying address lists, or for fees to be taken for a tenant simply registering their name, interest or requirements with an agent.
  • Most letting agents can charge fees, such as holding (reservation) fees, deposits and rent in advance. If you are unclear what you are being charged, or unsure as to whether you should pay a fee, seek advice or refer to the Tenant Fees Act 2019.

Temporary accommodation

Temporary accommodation is accommodation you should use on a temporary, short-term basis, such as a hotel, guest house or bed and breakfast. There are a number of private hotels and bed and breakfast businesses in Reading – the first place to search is online.

Homestay

Homestay accommodation is for students looking to stay with local, host families.

There is no Homestay service operated by the University of Reading or the Students’ Union. If you want to consider this option, you would need to look for an external supplier. A search on the internet can provide details of websites which match host families with people looking for homestay accommodation.

Some things to consider

  • The location of the homestay may not be as convenient, as it is likely that they will be offered all over Reading and the surrounding area: you will need to factor in the cost of travel as well as accommodation.
  • You will be expected to pay rent which will include bills, but is unlikely to include use of the telephone.

Your rights are different

In a homestay you are likely to be considered an ‘excluded occupier’ or ‘lodger’, which gives you fewer rights and less protection from eviction than in other private renting situations.

Talk to an adviser

If you would like further advice or to speak with somebody in person then a Students’ Union Adviser may be able to help. The specialist advisers offer a confidential service, independent from the University and can discuss your case with you in confidence.

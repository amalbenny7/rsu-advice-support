Council Tax

This information is for both Undergraduate and Postgraduate Students unless stated otherwise.

Do students have to pay?

Firstly, it is important to be aware that Council Tax is a serious matter and local authorities regularly take people to the Magistrates Court for non-payment. It is important to check the rules that apply to you and contact the local authority if you think they have made a mistake. Do not ignore a letter from the local authority concerning Council Tax, even if you do not think you are liable to pay.

To be exempt from paying Council Tax, a student needs to be enrolled for the purpose of undertaking a “full-time course of education” for at least 24 weeks in an academic year. It is not necessary to count how many hours you – as an individual - study. If in doubt, ask your School if your course is a “full-time course of education”.

Students are considered “full-time” from the day they enrol on a “full-time” programme until the day they complete it, abandon it or are dismissed from it. This includes Easter and Christmas holidays during your course and Summer holidays between academic years, but does not include:

  • Any period before you enrol on the first year of your course. If you are an international student and arrive in the UK before you enrol, you will be liable to pay Council Tax until the date of your enrolment;
  • the Summer period after you complete your course

The following information outlines various situations of students at Reading, and Council Tax liability within these circumstances. If your situation is different to one of those listed, speak to our Adviser, who will clarify your Council Tax liability for you.

If you live in University Halls of Residence

You will be exempt from Council Tax, no matter what your status.

If you live in Private Halls of Residence

Different rules may apply, and you will need to provide evidence of your full-time student status to receive the exemption. Seek advice from the Advice Service if you are unclear.

If you are living in private rented accommodation

The situation concerning Council Tax can differ depending on make-up of the property. As a student, you would not be liable for Council Tax, but any non-students in the property may be. The table below outlines the common scenarios.

All full-time students All Full Time Students are exempt · No Council Tax is paid · This is known as a ‘Class N’ property
Majority of full-time students, one person not considered a full-time student All Full Time Students are exempt · The single non-student (or part time student) is liable. The single non-student or part-time student can apply for a Single Person Discount (25% reduction on the Council Tax Bill)
Majority are not considered full-time students, one full-time student The full-time students would be exempt from Council Tax liability · 100% of the Council Tax should be payable by other residence of the property
Even split between students and non-students All full-time students are exempt from Council Tax liability · 100% of Council Tax should be payable by anyone without full-time student status · No Single Person Discount can be applied, as there is more than one non-student in the property
Student status or mode of study

You might be unsure of your liability for Council Tax if your circumstances are slightly different from simply being a full-time student. We have outlined some scenarios below which can impact your liability for Council Tax, and what your liability would be in these different situations. If you are unsure, then please speak to the Advice Service, who can help you clarify the situation. Do not ignore letters from the local authority about Council Tax, even if you think they have made a mistake.

Part-time students You will be liable to pay Council Tax as you are not enrolled on a “full-time course (unless you live in a Student Halls of Residence – see above)
Students who are registered on a Full-time course but who are studying on a Part-time basis It is our understanding that students who are studying on a Part-Time basis but who are registered on a Full-Time course are not considered to be exempt from Council Tax by local authorities in this area.
Full-time pre-sessional students If you are studying in this way and would like to discuss your Council Tax liability, please speak to an Adviser. students Pre-sessional students also need to prove enrolment on a full-time course in order to be exempt from paying Council Tax: you will not be able to do this if your course is for less than 24 weeks, even if you are going on to study on a full-time course immediately after your pre-sessional course.
Full-time students on period(s) of required placement If you have any questions seek advice from our Adviser.If you are on a sandwich course or a placement required by your course, you should be exempt from paying Council Tax during these periods, as you remain enrolled on a full-time course.
Full-time students who suspend their studies The local council may consider that being suspended means a student does not meet the definition of a full-time student, and therefore you are liable to pay Council Tax. Some authorities consider the reasons for suspension when deciding liability to pay.
Any student between the end of one course and the start of another If you are in this situation please speak to our Adviser. If you complete one programme and are due to start a separate programme, you will be liable to pay Council Tax between these courses. The exception to this is if you are living in University Halls of Residence – note that separate rules apply to Private Halls of Residence
Full-time Postgraduate Students who extend their studies beyond their original end date (for example, when writing up) In these circumstances, some Councils do not deem that writing-up students meet the definition of a full-time student. If you are charged Council Tax under these circumstances, seek advice from our Advice Service, but be aware that you may be liaiable to pay. You may need to give further information on your status/circumstances to your local authority, e.g. a letter from the Graduate School.
International and EU Students You should be aware that you may be liable to pay Council Tax if:
  • You arrive in the UK before you are enrolled onto a course;
  • You remain in rented accommodation after the end of your course and before returning to your home country.
Dependents or spouse of International Students If the spouse/dependant is not a British citizen, and, as a condition of their visa is one of the following
  • not permitted to work OR
  • not granted ‘recourse to public funds’
they should not be liable to pay. The dependant should provide evidence (a photocopy of their visa) to the local authority. Please be aware that only one adult dependent is exempt per each visa.
How do I let the Council know that I am a student?

When you move into a property, you should contact the local authority to claim exemption from Council Tax. You can obtain a “Council Tax Exemption Certificate” or “Certificate of Enrolment” from the Student Helpdesk in the Carrington Building or by logging onto your RISIS Account, selecting ‘Self Service Letters’ and printing your enrolment letter. You should then send this to your local authority.

Don’t assume that the local authority will know that you are eligible for an exemption: if in doubt, speak to your local authority.

Failure to pay Council Tax – the consequences

If you are sent a Council Tax bill and you have not paid it, the local authority will send you a ‘Reminder to Pay’ letter. This may be a demand for the full annual amount to be paid. Even if you consider yourself to be exempt, if you ignore Council Tax bills and reminder letters, the local authority may take further action against you. This could be a Magistrates’ Court summons, which may also lead to additional Court fees.

If the court grants the local authority the power to recover the debt from you, this may result in:

  • A visit from an Enforcement Agency, to take away your possessions up to the value of your debt;
  • An attachment to earnings or benefits order – this allows your employer to take money straight from your wages to pay back to the Council;
  • Up to three months imprisonment.

It is important not to ignore any letters from your Local Authority concerning Council Tax, even if you think that you are exempt from paying

If you receive a letter but are not sure what it means, or what you should do about it, speak to a member of our Advice Service to try and resolve the matter before any of the above actions are taken.

Financial implications of withdrawing or suspending from University, and Compelling Personal Reasons

Withdrawal or suspending, and Student Finance England

Your entitlement to the Student Finance package will stop on the day that you are withdrawn/suspended from your course.

This means that you will need to pay back any money which was given to you for after that date. Student Finance England considers that students who have suspended or withdrawn now have the time to be in full- time employment and therefore do not need a maintenance loan/grant.

Tuition Fee Loan

Depending on the point in the academic year you leave your course will depend on how much of your tuition fees you will need to repay

Fee liability points (PDF, University of Reading)

Maintenance Loan

If you have received your maintenance loan and left your course within the period that this was set to cover, this period will be considered an overpayment. This sum will be repaid differently to the rest of your Student Loan which would be repaid as per the terms of your loan when you took it out.

Students will have the option to either:

  • Have the overpayment deducted from their next Student Finance instalment if you are enrolling on a new course or repeating a year;
  • Pay it back directly to the Student Loans Company

Students who are suspending on medical grounds get an additional 60 days funding applied to their account from the day they left their course.

You will need to apply for a medical suspension through the University.

Withdrawal or suspending, and Council Tax

Full time students are usually exempt from Council Tax. However, if a student leaves their course they may become liable if they are living in privately rented accommodation.

For Council Tax purposes, the definition of a full time student is one whose course:

  • lasts for at least one calendar or academic year of duration;
  • runs for at least 24 weeks within that year;
  • includes 21 hours (or more) of study a week during term time.

If a student receives a Council Tax bill they need to make sure

  1. The period of liability is correct;
  2. If you are the only person in the house liable for Council Tax that a single person discount has been applied – this is a 25% reduction.

Compelling Personal Reasons (CPR)

If you have been unable to finish a year at University due to reasons outside your control, you can apply to have that year disregarded by a process called ‘Compelling Personal Reasons’ (CPR) by Student Finance England. Examples of where this may apply include if you have suspended due to illness, a family bereavement or caring responsibilities.

Entitlement with Student Finance is only for the tuition fee loan, there isn’t a restriction for the amounts of years you can apply for maintenance loan.

Student Finance England will fund the Length of your current course + one additional year, with previous years of study deducted. Previous study is related to courses you may have undertaken before the course you are currently enrolled on.

To request CPR, you must write a letter to Student Finance England which explains how your CPR affected your studies at the time and provide evidence of this. The letter should contain;

  • Your details (name, address, university and course titles and Customer Reference Number);
  • Your Student Finance history e.g. when you started your course, which year the CPR needs to be applied to;
  • What the reasons for your CPR request are and how your CPR has affected you and your studies.

Evidence can include:

  • Medical letters (GP or specialists);
  • Letters from University services like Counselling and Wellbeing or Disability Advisory Service or your Personal Tutor;
  • Other: Police report/Crime Reference Number or death certificate etc.

Please note that if you are granted CPR then you are still liable to pay back all of the money which has been loaned to you once you graduate even if a year is disregarded.

Council Tax

If you live in private rented accommodation, it is likely that your course will finish before the end of your tenancy agreement. For example, if term ends on the 9th June whilst most tenancies finish on 30th June. As a final year student, for the last few weeks of your tenancy you are no longer classed as a full-time student for the purpose of Council Tax, and you will be liable to pay.

It is important to pay the tax in a timely manner to avoid extra charges or court summons.

It is likely that the local authority will send letters or emails about Council Tax, and these may not be sent to the address they relate: it is important not to ignore any letters about Council Tax, even if you do not think you have to pay.

Leaving your accommodation: deposits and bills

Make sure to settle all final utility bills before leaving your rented accommodation. Inform your utility, water and internet provider on the date that you are leaving the property. Ensure you take meter readings on the last day of your tenancy as these will be used to calculate your final bill.

Arranging a check-out of your property is important so that your landlord receives your inventory and checks the property when your tenancy is over so that you are not at risk of losing your deposit.

If you have left your belongings in your university accommodation, companies who will pack and forward your belongings at a charge exist.

Bank accounts

When you have a student bank account with your provider, you are often offered great deals such as free overdrafts and railcards. When you complete your studies and your account switches, these offers may change. Check your account’s terms and conditions or speak to your bank if you are not sure what these are. It may even be advantageous to change banks when your course ends to take advantage of different offers, such as 0% overdrafts for up to three years.

Student funding

Undergraduate students

An undergraduate student is someone studying their first degree-level qualification, such as a Bachelor’s degree (BA, BSc, LLB, or BEng). This includes those who are studying integrated masters and PGCE students.

There are exceptions for those who are completing their second degrees and criteria for who would receive funding if studying part-time here: www.gov.uk/student-finance/who-qualifies

You apply for student finance ahead of the start of the course – your application is made to the area where you lived before you studied. You will need to have lived in the UK for 3 years before the start of the course to qualify as a home student.

  • Student Finance England: www.gov.uk/student-finance
  • Student Finance Wales: Student Finance Wales
  • Student Finance Northern Ireland: Student Finance NI
  • Student Awards Agency Scotland (SAAS): SAAS Funding
Students without parental support

Student Finance England generally considers students aged 25 or over to be independent for student finance assessments. While students over 21 might be considered by the university to be mature – students between 21-25 may still need to submit parent’s information unless you meet one of the other criteria.

Independent students are assessed for student finance based on their own circumstances rather than their parents’ income; to apply for this you would need to have evidence that shows you financially supported yourself for 3 years. You would also be considered independent if you are under 25 and a parent, married or in a civil partnership.

Estranged students are those who no longer have contact with their parents due to a permanent breakdown in the relationship. You will need to give evidence about the relationship breakdown and from a professional who is aware of the situation. Contact Reading Student Union Advice if you have any questions.

For more information, please see:

  • Standalone: www.standalone.org.uk
  • UCAS estranged information: www.ucas.com/applying/applying-to-university/students-with-individual-needs/estranged-students
Disabled students

Disabled students may be able to access additional support through Disabled Students’ Allowance (DSA), which can help fund specialist equipment, non-medical support, study-related travel costs, and other disability-related study needs.

If you need help applying for DSA or discussing support available at the University of Reading, contact the Disability Advisory Service.

For information about student finance and DSA eligibility visit www.gov.uk/disabled-students-allowance-dsa

International students

You would be considered an international student if you come to the UK to study and do not qualify for UK home fee status. International students can access specialist support with visas and immigration, settling into life in the UK, employment, finances, wellbeing, and academic life through the University’s International Student Advisory Team and Global Study lounge. Please note you can be charged home fees from the university and still be seen as an international student by student finance, please use the links below to do your research on home fee status.

  • University of Reading International Student Advisory Team for visa, immigration and general student support.
  • Global Study Lounge for social activities, language support, cultural integration and community building.
  • UKCISA (UK Council for International Student Affairs) for independent advice on visas, fees, employment and student rights.

If you are an international student looking for more information on bursaries and funding please look at the following sites:

  • The Scholarship Hub for a searchable database of UK scholarships and bursaries.
  • Prospects Scholarships Search for undergraduate and postgraduate funding opportunities.
  • study-uk.britishcouncil.org/scholarships-funding for scholarships funded by universities, charities and government schemes.
Postgraduate students

A postgraduate student is someone who has already completed an undergraduate degree and is studying at a more advanced level, such as a Master’s degree, Postgraduate Certificate, Postgraduate Diploma, or PhD.

You can find information about funding from student finance for postgraduate loans on the link below including application criteria, repayment threshold.

Please be aware that the master’s loan is expected to cover tuition fees, you would be expected to make plans to cover living costs before starting the course.

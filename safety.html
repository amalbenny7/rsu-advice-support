Safety

We are committed to creating a safe and inclusive students’ union, a place for everyone.

We have a zero-tolerance policy to sexual harassment, discrimination and violence. If you ever feel uncomfortable in our venue, find a member of staff and ask for Angela or Clive.

In an emergency

If you are in immediate danger or at immediate risk of harm

Call the Police on 999. On campus, you can also call Campus Security on 0118 378 6300.

If you are in the Students’ Union building and need help urgently, ask for Angela or Clive at the bar and tell a member of staff. We will then help you contact the relevant support.

  • Police – emergency 999
  • Campus Security 0118 378 6300
  • Counselling & Wellbeing on campus Carrington Building · 0118 378 4216 · for mental health or emotional difficulties

Help on campus and in our venue

Ask for Angela

People who feel unsafe, vulnerable or threatened can discreetly seek help by approaching venue staff and asking them for ‘Angela’. This code-phrase will indicate to staff that they require help with their situation, and a trained member of staff will then look to support and assist them. This might be through reuniting them with a friend, seeing them to a taxi, or by calling venue security and/or the police.

Ask for Clive

Ask for Clive is a charity that partners with venues to promote inclusion and to create welcoming environments for the LGBTQ+ community. They work directly with local communities and venues, and collaborate with law enforcement on training and reporting initiatives. Our venue is part of the Ask for Clive venues – everyone is welcome at Reading Students’ Union, and we take a zero-tolerance approach to harassment of any kind.

Sexual assault and consent

Sexual assault can affect anyone, regardless of their identity. It does not matter the gender, the sexuality, sexual history, or anything else of the individual. Sexual assault is the result of one thing: a lack of consent.

Consent can never be assumed. The easiest way to make sure consent is given is to ask before sexual activity occurs. If the person freely responds ‘yes’ (without force or pressure), this is consent. Remember, it’s your body and only you can decide what you do with it – Safe Sex Berkshire.

If you have been assaulted

Sexual Assault Referral Centres are available 24 hours a day.
